of SRAM & eDRAM from 65 nm to 22 nm

Motivation & Scope

Three-dimensional die stacking promises to halve memory footprint through vertical integration, but the net benefit depends critically on process node, cell topology, and the TSV technology assumptions embedded in the design model. For an SRAM cache, 3D stacking consistently reduces per-die area because the 6T cell's large baseline absorbs TSV keep-out overhead. For eDRAM, whose compact 1T-1C cell already occupies less silicon, the same TSV overhead can increase total footprint at large feature sizes.

We use DESTINY's analytical cache model to address three questions across four memory technologies (planar and stacked variants of SRAM and eDRAM) and four process nodes (65, 45, 32, 22 nm):

  • How do area, latency, write energy, and leakage scale with node for each technology?
  • When does 3D stacking benefit eDRAM, and what determines the crossover point?
  • How sensitive are system-level metrics to TSV projection and redundancy assumptions?

Conclusions

1
3D eDRAM is Pareto-optimal on area, read latency, and write energy at 32 nm simultaneously. At 22 nm its footprint reaches 0.55 mm² — 22× more compact than 2D SRAM at 65 nm. Its read latency of 0.42 ns is the only monotonically improving trajectory across all nodes.
2
2D eDRAM is the leakage-optimal choice at every node, owing to its 1T-1C cell topology contributing a single subthreshold leakage path per bit versus six in SRAM's 6T cell. The structural leakage advantage is 5–8× across all nodes and persists regardless of optimization target.
3
The 65 nm SRAM latency spike (9.94 ns) and the 32 nm SRAM leakage spike (234.6 mW) are both WriteEDP optimizer artifacts — not technology-intrinsic limits. A ReadLatency or LeakagePower target would yield substantially different results at these nodes.
4
TSV technology uncertainty has minimal system-level impact at the 2 MB cache scale. Conservative TSV projections incur at most +5.5% area and +6.7% write energy, with zero latency penalty — the critical path does not traverse inter-die TSV interconnect at this configuration.
5
3D stacking benefits SRAM at all nodes (−42 to −50% area) but introduces an area overhead for eDRAM at 65 nm (+30%), with the crossover occurring between 65 and 45 nm. TSV keep-out area is non-trivial relative to the compact 1T-1C cell at large feature sizes.

3D eDRAM area crossover between 65 and 45 nm At 65 nm, TSV keep-out zones exceed the die-halving benefit for the compact 1T-1C cell (7.74 mm² vs. 5.96 mm² for 2D eDRAM). By 45 nm, the same TSV overhead represents a smaller fraction of the reduced cell area, and 3D stacking delivers a 31% footprint reduction.

Cache Hit Latency vs. Process Node

Fig. 2 — 3D eDRAM is the sole technology with strictly monotonic latency improvement (0.54→0.42 ns). SRAM exhibits an optimizer-induced discontinuity at 65 nm that disappears at 45 nm.
65 nm SRAM latency spike — optimizer artifact, not a technology ceiling The 2D SRAM array at 65 nm spans 12.35 mm². DESTINY's WriteEDP optimizer responds by selecting a deeply hierarchical bank organization to reduce energy, forcing global H-tree wire delays (t ∝ √A) to dominate: 9.94 ns for 2D SRAM, 4.70 ns for 3D SRAM. At 45 nm, the 30% area reduction collapses H-tree depth and latency drops to sub-1 ns. Under a ReadLatency target, this discontinuity would not appear.
Post-45 nm SRAM latency increases mildly (0.94→1.18 ns) Under WriteEDP, the optimizer progressively favors energy-conservative configurations at finer nodes, accepting slightly higher latency. 3D eDRAM avoids this because its compact per-die footprint allows the optimizer to minimize both terms simultaneously.

Total Leakage Power vs. Process Node

Fig. 3 — eDRAM leakage is 5–8× lower than SRAM at all nodes. SRAM spikes to 234.6 mW at 32 nm (both 2D and 3D converge to the same value).
32 nm SRAM leakage spike: 234.6 mW — identical for 2D and 3D The matching values confirm the WriteEDP optimizer selected the same physical configuration regardless of die stacking. At 32 nm, minimizing write EDP requires a high-subarray-parallelism organization that dramatically increases simultaneously biased transistors. eDRAM is immune: its 1T-1C cell contributes one leakage transistor per bit vs. six for SRAM.

Write Dynamic Energy vs. Process Node

Fig. 4 — SRAM energy peaks at 45 nm (reorganization artifact). All technologies converge to 0.019–0.035 nJ at 22 nm as VDD² scaling dominates.
Non-monotonic SRAM energy: configuration reorganization, not physics Ewrite = Csw · VDD² · α. At 45 nm, the optimizer selects higher subarray parallelism (lowering write latency, improving EDP), increasing Csw enough to reverse the VDD² reduction. By 32–22 nm, voltage scaling reasserts dominance and all technologies converge.
